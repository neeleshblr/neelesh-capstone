Eurobites: Swiss telcos get cozy with RAN sharing

Also in today's EMEA regional roundup: MTN grows service revenue and subscribers in H1; Cell C switches on 5G; Telefónica nets satellite defense deal.

Michelle Donegan,Senior Editor, Light Reading

August 24, 2026

3 Min Read
(Source: VogelSP/Alamy Stock Photo)

Rival Swiss telcos Salt and Sunrise are considering deeper collaboration on mobile infrastructure to improve coverage in rural and less populated areas with "greater financial efficiency." They already work together at some mobile sites and signed a memorandum of understanding to assess potential for "closer cooperation" in the radio access network (RAN) via multi-operator core network (MOCN) sharing. In this model, they would share antenna sites but operate their own core networks. The next steps are to evaluate the technical, operational and regulatory requirements. They noted that a final agreement is subject to the outcome of this study phase as well as regulatory approvals.

Data drives MTN Group growth in H1

Pan-African mobile operator MTN Group grew its customer base by 6.7% to 317.7 million across 19 markets as of the end of June, while the number of active data subscribers increased 9.1% to 179.3 million in the first half of 2026. The Group reported service revenue of R115.3 billion (US$7.2 billion) in H1, up 9.7% on a reported basis and up 17.5% in constant currency terms, driven mainly by growth in data services with a boost from fintech as well. EBITDA (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization) increased 20% to R56 billion ($3.5 billion) on a reported basis. The operator said it is making "good progress" on the transaction to acquire 75% of tower company IHS that it does not already own, which is expected to close in the second half of this year. (See MTN in talks to acquire tower company IHS.)

Related:Private wireless RAN growth slows to mid-single digits in 1H26 – Dell'Oro

Cell C goes live with 5G

MTN's South African competitor Cell C reportedly has 5G up and running, although it has not yet launched a commercial proposition for the service, according to Tech Central. It is the country's fourth-largest mobile operator and has roaming agreements with MTN and Vodacom. Cell C also told Tech Central that it is talking with Starlink, Amazon Leo and others about reselling their satellite broadband services as well as the possibility of working on a direct-to-device offering, although Starlink is not licensed to operate in South Africa. (See Cell C gains 1.3M subscribers, reduces debt.)

Telefónica lands satellite defense deal

Telefónica has won a €5.15 million ($6 million) contract with Spain's Ministry of Defense to provide satellite communications infrastructure, according to Expansión. The Spanish operator's role under the framework agreement is to deliver systems that enable multiple users to share satellite network capacity. The operator recently teamed with Spanish satellite IoT specialist Sateliot to develop 5G non-terrestrial network technology for applications including in security and defense. (See Eurobites: Telefónica, Sateliot combine on 5G IoT.)

Vodafone franchise fallout sparks UK review

The UK government is looking into how franchise businesses are regulated, following allegations of misconduct such as those seen in a long-running legal dispute with Vodafone. The government review could result in new legislation to better protect franchisees, according to The Guardian. In Vodafone UK's case, the operator settled its dispute with former store franchisees in July this year. The claim against the operator was launched in late 2024 by a group of more than 60 former Vodafone store franchisees who were seeking more than £120 million ($161 million) from the operator as compensation for what they said were "irrational and arbitrary business decisions" – such as drastically cutting commission payments – that resulted in the franchisees experiencing severe financial, mental and physical distress. (See Eurobites: Former Vodafone franchisees step up fight for justice.)
